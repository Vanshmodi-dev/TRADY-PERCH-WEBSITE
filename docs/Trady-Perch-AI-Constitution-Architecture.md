# TRADY PERCH AI CONSTITUTION
## Architecture & Table of Contents — Version 1.0

**This is not the AI Constitution. This is its blueprint.**

> The Master Vision Document is the Constitution. The Design System Bible is the Statute of appearance. The Motion Bible is the Statute of movement. The UX / Experience Blueprint is the Statute of sequence and journey. The Brand Identity Manual is the Statute of recognition across medium. The Product Implementation Constitution — operating, inside Trady Perch, as the Engineering Constitution named in this document's own charter — is the Statute of how software gets built. Between them, those six documents answer what Trady Perch looks like, how it moves, what a human feels while using it, how it is recognized everywhere it appears, and how it is engineered. None of them answers the question this document exists to answer: when the thing doing the looking, moving, recognizing, and engineering is not a human but an AI system acting on Trady Perch's behalf, what governs *it*? Not its interface. Not its output's typography. Its judgment. This is the seventh and most structurally unusual document in Trady Perch's constitutional hierarchy — unusual because its subject is not a static artifact a person reviews before shipping, but a class of systems that reason, decide, and act continuously, often faster than any human review cycle can keep pace with. Every other document in this hierarchy governs something that holds still long enough to be specified. This one governs something that, by design, does not.

## PART ZERO — HOW THIS ARCHITECTURE WORKS

### 0.1 The Inheritance Protocol

The authority order, stated once here and true everywhere below: **Master Vision Document → Design System Bible → Motion Bible → UX / Experience Blueprint → Brand Identity Manual → Product Implementation Constitution (Engineering Constitution) → AI Constitution.** Every chapter in this Constitution must open with an "Inherited From" citation, exactly as each document above it already requires of itself. A chapter that cannot name what it inherits has not earned a place here — it is either an accident of whichever AI research trend happened to be ascendant that quarter, or it belongs in one of the six documents above it instead.

A note on naming, resolved once so it never needs re-litigating in any chapter below: the brief that charters this document lists "Product Implementation Constitution" and "Engineering Constitution" as two distinct upstream documents, sixth and seventh in Trady Perch's hierarchy. As built, Trady Perch has one document occupying that operational role — the Product Implementation Constitution — whose own charter already states that it is "the electrical code and the building inspector" for everything engineered at Trady Perch. This architecture treats "Engineering Constitution" as that document's functional name, not a separate eighth artifact to be invented without a mandate. If Trady Perch's engineering organization later grows large enough to warrant splitting implementation standards from a narrower engineering-culture document, this Constitution's inheritance table is the first place that split must be reflected.

This Constitution's distinct job, stated precisely so its 118 chapters don't quietly re-litigate work already settled above: it does not decide what a chat bubble looks like (Design System Bible), how it animates into view (Motion Bible), why the AI is speaking at that moment in the journey (UX Blueprint), what the AI's persona is named and how it is drawn (Brand Identity Manual), or which repository the code implementing it lives in (Product Implementation Constitution). It decides what the AI is allowed to *think*, *decide*, *remember*, *say*, *withhold*, *escalate*, *refuse*, and *learn* — the interior governance of every reasoning process Trady Perch fields, whether that process is a customer-facing chat assistant, an autonomous coding agent, or a foundation model Trady Perch has not yet built a product around. Six documents describe the building, the code that builds it, and the electrician who wires it; this one is the character of the mind now allowed to walk through the finished rooms unsupervised.

That asymmetry is this document's entire reason for existing, and Part VII (AI Safety) and Part XII (Governance) exist specifically to keep it from becoming a liability.

### 0.2 How to Read Each Chapter Entry

Every chapter below carries exactly the thirteen fields requested, in fixed order, several combined onto shared lines where their content is naturally short, exactly as the Product Implementation Constitution and UX / Experience Blueprint architectures already establish as house style:

- **Chapter Name** — the chapter's number and title, as a heading.
- **Purpose** — the one-sentence job the chapter does, stated as a governing rule an AI system or its builder can act on, not an aspiration.
- **Business Importance** — what the chapter protects or wins commercially: cost, trust, liability, differentiation, or velocity.
- **AI Importance** — what the chapter changes about how an AI system actually reasons, decides, or behaves differently than it would with no governing standard at all.
- **Ethical Importance** — what harm, to a user, a third party, or the information ecosystem the chapter's absence would risk, and why that harm matters independent of business consequence.
- **Dependencies** — which other chapters in this Constitution, or specific sections of the six documents above it, must exist first, conceptually, before this chapter can be written without contradiction.
- **Future Dependencies** — the not-yet-true condition — a shipped product surface, a new model capability, a regulatory change, an organizational milestone — whose arrival would trigger this chapter's next material revision, named so the chapter's authors know what to watch for rather than guessing when "done" quietly became "outdated."
- **Cross-References** — the specific Master Vision, Design System Bible, Motion Bible, UX Blueprint, Brand Identity Manual, and Product Implementation Constitution chapters/sections this chapter operationalizes.
- **Estimated Page Count** — a page range at the rigor already established by the six documents above this one.
- **Suggested Writing Order** — a global sequence number, frequently different from reading-order position, reflecting which chapters must be drafted first so later chapters can cite settled doctrine instead of inventing it inline.
- **Complexity Level** — Low, Medium, High, or Very High: a qualitative estimate of how much genuinely unresolved judgment (as opposed to transcription of upstream doctrine) the chapter requires.
- **Review Requirements** — which standing body or role (AI Review Board, Legal & Compliance, Security, a named upstream document's owner, external red-team) must sign off before the chapter is considered authoritative, not merely drafted.
- **Success Criteria** — a falsifiable test for whether the eventual chapter did its job: something a reviewer, an automated eval suite, or an AI agent itself could actually check, not a feeling.

### 0.3 Why the Parts Are Grouped This Way

Twelve Parts, sequenced as the order a governing mind actually has to be built in — first who it is, then how it is structured, then how it thinks, then how it acts, then how it remembers, then how it is kept safe, then how it is experienced, then how it is checked, then how each specific role built from it must behave, then how it changes, then how it is kept honest over time:

- **Part I — AI Philosophy & First Principles** answers *"what kind of mind is this, before a single agent exists?"* — the direct extension of Master Vision Ch. 19 into a full doctrine.
- **Part II — Agent Architecture** answers *"what is the shape of the system this philosophy has to be built into?"* — single agents, multi-agent systems, roles, hierarchies, and the memory and context substrate they run on.
- **Part III — Reasoning Standards** answers *"how does it think, once built?"* — the cognitive discipline underneath every output, whether or not a human ever sees the reasoning itself.
- **Part IV — Prompt Engineering** answers *"how is that reasoning instructed, reliably, by the humans and systems responsible for it?"*
- **Part V — Tool Usage** answers *"how does thought become action in the world outside the model?"* — the highest-consequence Part in this document, since a tool call, unlike a sentence, can be irreversible.
- **Part VI — Memory Systems** answers *"what does it carry forward, and under what discipline?"* — placed after reasoning and tools deliberately, since memory only matters once there is reasoning and action for it to inform.
- **Part VII — AI Safety** answers *"what stops any of the above from causing harm?"* — the non-negotiable floor beneath every other Part, cited by nearly all of them.
- **Part VIII — Human-AI Experience** answers *"what does all of this feel like to the person on the other end?"* — the direct behavioral counterpart to the UX / Experience Blueprint's Part VIII, made concrete rather than aspirational.
- **Part IX — AI Quality** answers *"how do we know any of the above is actually working, continuously, not just on the day it shipped?"*
- **Part X — Role-Specific AI Constitutions** answers *"what does all of the above mean, specifically, for the coding agent, the support agent, the sales agent, the internal employee agent?"* — deliberately placed after the abstract Parts it specializes, never before.
- **Part XI — Future Intelligence** answers *"what changes when the model itself changes — voice, vision, robotics, a capability Trady Perch does not yet have a product for?"*
- **Part XII — Governance** answers *"how does this Constitution itself stay honest as the models, the team, and the law change faster than any document can be rewritten?"* — deliberately last, mirroring the closing governance Part in every document above it, and the only Part explicitly designed to be revisited on a fixed calendar rather than only when something breaks.

### 0.4 Scale

118 numbered chapters across twelve Parts, plus front matter and three appendices, targeting **560–700 total pages** — inside the requested 500–800 range, weighted toward the lower-middle because a governance document for reasoning systems earns its authority through enforceable, testable rules, not through argumentative length for its own sake, even though its scope (spanning philosophy, architecture, safety, and nine distinct product-surface archetypes) is the broadest of the seven constitutional documents. At the authoring pace already established by the six documents above it, this is realistically a **26–34 month** undertaking — the longest in the hierarchy, for a reason none of the other six share: this document's subject matter changes underneath it faster than any subject the other six govern. A color token or a motion curve is stable once decided; a foundation model's capabilities are not. Several Parts (V, VII, IX, XI, XII) are written with the explicit expectation of a standing revision cadence rather than a one-time authoring pass, and Part XII exists specifically to make that cadence an obligation rather than a hope.

# PART I — AI PHILOSOPHY & FIRST PRINCIPLES

*Why this document exists before any agent does — the direct operational extension of Master Vision Ch. 19's AI Personality Constitution into a full governing philosophy. Every later chapter's judgment calls are checked against this Part first.*

### 1. The Purpose of Intelligence at Trady Perch
**Purpose:** State plainly why Trady Perch builds and deploys AI systems at all — to extend specific human capability and judgment, never to replace accountability for it — so every later capability decision is checked against a stated purpose rather than "because it is now possible."
**Business Importance:** Prevents capability-led product decisions that erode client trust in a premium, judgment-driven brand; anchors AI investment to the same restraint-first philosophy that already differentiates Trady Perch's design and engineering.
**AI Importance:** Gives every system built downstream a fixed north star to reason against when a novel situation has no explicit rule yet.
**Ethical Importance:** Without a stated purpose, capability naturally expands to fill whatever a model can technically do; naming the purpose is the first and cheapest safeguard against scope creep into manipulation, surveillance, or replacement of human accountability.
**Dependencies:** None — this is the root chapter. **Future Dependencies:** Revisit if Trady Perch enters a market (e.g., regulated finance, healthcare-adjacent) where "extend human judgment" requires a jurisdiction-specific redefinition.
**Cross-References:** MV Ch. 19 (§19.1–19.3); MV Ch. 27 & 30.
**Estimated Page Count:** 5–6 pg. **Suggested Writing Order:** #1. **Complexity Level:** Medium.
**Review Requirements:** Founder/Chief AI Officer sign-off; no delegation permitted for this chapter alone.
**Success Criteria:** A proposed AI feature can be approved or rejected by citing this chapter's purpose statement alone, before any technical feasibility discussion begins.

### 2. AI Principles
**Purpose:** Compress the full constitution's spirit into a small, memorizable set of principles an engineer, a designer, or an AI agent itself can hold in working memory mid-decision, exactly as the Product Implementation Constitution's Ch. 1 does for engineering.
**Business Importance:** A short principle set is what actually gets followed under deadline pressure; a 700-page document that is never internalized protects no one.
**AI Importance:** Serves as the literal system-prompt-level compression every deployed agent's instructions ultimately trace back to — the chapter most directly copy-pasted into production.
**Ethical Importance:** Principles stated once, clearly, and repeatedly cited are harder to quietly violate than principles buried in Part-specific detail no one rereads.
**Dependencies:** Ch. 1. **Future Dependencies:** Each principle must be re-tested against every new Part added to this Constitution; a principle a later Part cannot cite is either wrong or the later Part is.
**Cross-References:** MV Ch. 19 (§19.1–19.10); DSB Ch. 1 (Design System Principles, structural parallel).
**Estimated Page Count:** 6–8 pg. **Suggested Writing Order:** #2. **Complexity Level:** High.
**Review Requirements:** AI Review Board (Ch. 111) founding charter session.
**Success Criteria:** A new AI agent, given only this chapter, can correctly predict this Constitution's answer to a novel scenario none of its other 117 chapters explicitly addresses.

### 3. Human-Centered AI Doctrine
**Purpose:** Fix the non-negotiable relationship between an AI system's convenience and a human's actual agency — the human always retains the ability to understand, override, and opt out of AI-mediated decisions that affect them.
**Business Importance:** Protects Trady Perch from the reputational and regulatory exposure that comes from AI systems that quietly narrow a user's real choices while appearing to expand them.
**AI Importance:** Establishes the default posture — augment, disclose, and defer — that every subsequent autonomy decision in Part II and Part V is measured against.
**Ethical Importance:** The most direct safeguard in this Constitution against the specific harm of an AI system optimizing for engagement or task-completion at the expense of the human's actual interest.
**Dependencies:** Ch. 1–2. **Future Dependencies:** Revisit as agentic autonomy in Part X grows — a doctrine written for a chat assistant needs explicit re-derivation for a fully autonomous workflow agent.
**Cross-References:** MV Ch. 19 (§19.4); UX Blueprint Ch. 6, Ch. 73 (Human-AI Collaboration Model).
**Estimated Page Count:** 6–7 pg. **Suggested Writing Order:** #3. **Complexity Level:** High.
**Review Requirements:** AI Review Board; UX Blueprint owner (co-sign, given direct dependency).
**Success Criteria:** Every product surface in Part X can name, from this chapter alone, the specific override mechanism it must expose to remain compliant.

### 4. Trust as a Design Constraint
**Purpose:** Define trust not as a marketing outcome but as an engineering constraint — specific, checkable behaviors (consistent honesty about uncertainty, no manufactured urgency, no silent capability overreach) an AI system must exhibit to earn continued reliance.
**Business Importance:** Trust, once broken by a single AI hallucination or overreach presented confidently, is disproportionately expensive to rebuild relative to the cost of having prevented it; this chapter is the cheapest insurance in the document.
**AI Importance:** Converts "be trustworthy" from an unfalsifiable aspiration into specific behavioral tests Part IX's evaluation frameworks can actually run.
**Ethical Importance:** Misplaced trust in an AI system that has not earned it is itself a harm, independent of any single bad output — it changes how a human allocates their own diligence.
**Dependencies:** Ch. 1–3. **Future Dependencies:** Recalibrate as external AI-trust benchmarks and regulation (e.g., disclosure requirements for AI-generated content) mature past their current early state.
**Cross-References:** MV Ch. 19 (§19.5); UX Blueprint Ch. 3 (Psychology of Trust), Ch. 17 (Trust Architecture), Ch. 71 (Trust-in-AI Standard).
**Estimated Page Count:** 6–7 pg. **Suggested Writing Order:** #4. **Complexity Level:** High.
**Review Requirements:** AI Review Board; UX Blueprint owner.
**Success Criteria:** Every trust-signal claim an agent is permitted to make (e.g., "I've verified this") can be traced to an actual verification step this chapter requires, with zero cases of asserted-but-unverified trust language.

### 5. Transparency Standard
**Purpose:** Fix what an AI system must proactively disclose — that it is an AI, what data it used, what it is uncertain about — without requiring the user to ask.
**Business Importance:** Proactive disclosure is markedly cheaper, reputationally and legally, than disclosure extracted after a complaint or an investigation.
**AI Importance:** Defines the default disclosure surface (a persistent, unavoidable signal) every product surface in Part X inherits unless a specific chapter there argues for a narrower exception.
**Ethical Importance:** A user who does not know they are interacting with, or being affected by, an AI system cannot meaningfully consent to that interaction.
**Dependencies:** Ch. 3–4. **Future Dependencies:** Jurisdiction-specific AI-disclosure law is actively developing; this chapter's minimum bar must be re-checked against new statutes as Trady Perch enters new markets.
**Cross-References:** MV Ch. 19 (§19.6); Brand Manual Ch. 100 (AI-Native Brand Presence Philosophy).
**Estimated Page Count:** 5–6 pg. **Suggested Writing Order:** #6. **Complexity Level:** Medium.
**Review Requirements:** Legal & Compliance; AI Review Board.
**Success Criteria:** An external auditor reviewing any Trady Perch AI surface can identify, without being told in advance, that they are interacting with an AI system within the first exchange.

### 6. Explainability Standard
**Purpose:** Fix the minimum bar for an AI system to state *why* it produced a given output or decision, in terms a non-technical user can act on — distinct from transparency (that it is AI) in that this governs the reasoning behind a specific output.
**Business Importance:** Explainable decisions are contestable decisions; contestability is what keeps an AI-mediated error from becoming an unresolvable dispute.
**AI Importance:** Requires every agent architecture in Part II to retain, at minimum, the reasoning trace needed to answer "why" after the fact, even when that trace is not shown by default.
**Ethical Importance:** An unexplainable decision that affects a person materially (pricing, eligibility, prioritization) denies that person the ability to identify and correct an error made about them.
**Dependencies:** Ch. 5. **Future Dependencies:** Revisit as reasoning-model architectures (Ch. 102) make internal reasoning traces less directly legible even to their own builders.
**Cross-References:** MV Ch. 19 (§19.7); UX Blueprint Ch. 75 (AI Error & Failure Experience).
**Estimated Page Count:** 6–7 pg. **Suggested Writing Order:** #7. **Complexity Level:** High.
**Review Requirements:** AI Review Board; Legal & Compliance (for any decision with material consequence).
**Success Criteria:** For any AI-mediated decision a user disputes, a specific, previously-logged explanation can be produced within a fixed SLA, with zero cases of "the system cannot say why."

### 7. Decision-Making Philosophy
**Purpose:** Establish the general posture an AI system takes when a decision is genuinely ambiguous — defer to the human, choose the more reversible option, or act within a pre-approved boundary — before Part III's specific reasoning mechanics are defined.
**Business Importance:** A stated default posture prevents inconsistent, agent-by-agent improvisation under ambiguity, which is where most costly AI mistakes actually originate.
**AI Importance:** The direct philosophical parent of Ch. 29 (Decision Trees) and every autonomy-boundary decision in Part V.
**Ethical Importance:** Reversibility-biased defaults under ambiguity are the single cheapest structural protection against an AI system causing harm it cannot undo.
**Dependencies:** Ch. 1, Ch. 3. **Future Dependencies:** None identified; this is intended as one of the most stable chapters in the document.
**Cross-References:** MV §3.1 ("If in doubt, remove it"); PIC Ch. 31 (AI Code Generation Guardrails, structural parallel).
**Estimated Page Count:** 5–6 pg. **Suggested Writing Order:** #5. **Complexity Level:** Medium.
**Review Requirements:** AI Review Board.
**Success Criteria:** Given a novel ambiguous scenario absent from this document, two independently-briefed engineers predict the same default posture from this chapter alone.

### 8. Reasoning Philosophy
**Purpose:** State what Trady Perch considers "good reasoning" to look like in an AI system — traceable, falsifiable, and honest about its own confidence — as the philosophical root Part III's ten reasoning-standard chapters implement mechanically.
**Business Importance:** Differentiates Trady Perch's AI products on demonstrable reasoning quality rather than output fluency alone, a defensible moat as raw model capability commoditizes.
**AI Importance:** The single chapter every chapter in Part III must be traceable back to.
**Ethical Importance:** Fluent-sounding but unsound reasoning is more dangerous than obviously poor reasoning, because it is harder for a user to catch — this chapter is the first line of defense against exactly that failure mode.
**Dependencies:** Ch. 1–2. **Future Dependencies:** Revisit as new reasoning-model paradigms (extended thinking, tool-integrated reasoning) change what "traceable" can mean in practice.
**Cross-References:** MV Ch. 19 (§19.8); UX Blueprint Ch. 68 (AI Experience Philosophy).
**Estimated Page Count:** 6–7 pg. **Suggested Writing Order:** #8. **Complexity Level:** High.
**Review Requirements:** AI Review Board.
**Success Criteria:** Every chapter in Part III can cite a specific clause of this chapter as its justification, with zero orphaned reasoning-standard chapters.

### 9. AI Ethics Foundations
**Purpose:** State the ethical commitments specific to AI systems that go beyond general business ethics — non-manipulation, non-deception, fairness across users, and respect for the boundary between influence and coercion.
**Business Importance:** Positions Trady Perch's AI products for durability under regulatory scrutiny that is tightening, not loosening, across every major market Trady Perch could plausibly expand into.
**AI Importance:** The direct upstream source Part VII's safety chapters translate into enforceable, testable mechanisms.
**Ethical Importance:** This is the chapter, more than any other, where the document's ethical seriousness is either genuine or performative — every later chapter's ethical claims are checked against whether they are actually entailed by this one.
**Dependencies:** Ch. 1, Ch. 3–4. **Future Dependencies:** Revisit against emerging AI-ethics regulation (EU AI Act-adjacent frameworks, sector-specific US rules) as they finalize.
**Cross-References:** MV Ch. 27 & 30; UX Blueprint Ch. 93 (Experience Ethics Doctrine), Ch. 99 (Ethics Consistency & Enforcement).
**Estimated Page Count:** 8–10 pg. **Suggested Writing Order:** #9. **Complexity Level:** Very High.
**Review Requirements:** AI Review Board; Legal & Compliance; external ethics advisor if/when Trady Perch retains one.
**Success Criteria:** Every safety mechanism in Part VII can be traced to a specific ethical commitment in this chapter it exists to enforce, with zero mechanisms that exist for compliance-optics reasons alone.

### 10. AI Limitations & Honest Incapacity
**Purpose:** Enumerate, and require every deployed system to be able to state, what Trady Perch's AI systems are not capable of and should never claim to be — the deliberate inverse of a capability roadmap.
**Business Importance:** Overclaiming AI capability is the single most common cause of AI product trust collapse industry-wide; this chapter is a direct structural defense against Trady Perch repeating that pattern.
**AI Importance:** Establishes the "I don't know" and "I can't do that" response patterns every agent must default to rather than confabulate around a genuine capability gap.
**Ethical Importance:** A system that hides its own limitations transfers risk onto the user silently, without their consent.
**Dependencies:** Ch. 8–9. **Future Dependencies:** This chapter has the shortest realistic shelf life in Part I — model capability genuinely changes month to month, and Ch. 118's revision cadence must apply to it explicitly.
**Cross-References:** MV Ch. 19 (§19.9); UX Blueprint Ch. 75 (AI Error & Failure Experience).
**Estimated Page Count:** 5–6 pg. **Suggested Writing Order:** #10. **Complexity Level:** Medium.
**Review Requirements:** AI Review Board; re-review triggered on every major foundation-model upgrade.
**Success Criteria:** A red-team prompt specifically designed to elicit an overclaim of capability is refused or honestly caveated in 100% of tested cases.

### 11. Anti-Philosophy: How Intelligence Fails Silently
**Purpose:** The deliberate inverse of Ch. 1–10 — a taxonomy of exactly how AI systems fail without anyone noticing until damage is done: confident hallucination, sycophantic agreement, silent scope creep, and optimization for the metric instead of the goal.
**Business Importance:** Naming failure modes in advance is dramatically cheaper than discovering them in production after a client-facing incident.
**AI Importance:** Gives Part IX's evaluation frameworks a concrete, non-hypothetical list of failure signatures to test against, closing Part I with the same "what this must never become" clarity every document above it gives its own domain.
**Ethical Importance:** Silent failure is worse than loud failure — this chapter exists specifically because the harms this Constitution is most worried about are the ones that don't announce themselves.
**Dependencies:** Ch. 1–10, in full. **Future Dependencies:** Living document by design — new silent-failure modes discovered in production or red-teaming (Ch. 73) must be added here, not merely fixed and forgotten.
**Cross-References:** DSB Ch. 68 (Anti-Pattern Library, structural parallel); PIC Ch. 5 (Anti-Philosophy, structural parallel).
**Estimated Page Count:** 6–8 pg. **Suggested Writing Order:** #11, closing Part I. **Complexity Level:** High.
**Review Requirements:** AI Review Board; Ch. 73's red-team function is a standing contributor to this chapter's living updates.
**Success Criteria:** An incident retrospective (Ch. 115) can always locate the failure mode it is analyzing as an already-named entry in this chapter, or the retrospective's first action item is adding it here.

# PART II — AGENT ARCHITECTURE

*The structural shape the philosophy in Part I has to be built into — from a single agent's boundaries to a fleet of agents coordinating without a human in every loop.*

### 12. Agent Architecture Philosophy
**Purpose:** Establish the general bias this Constitution holds toward architectural complexity — a single well-scoped agent is preferred over a multi-agent system unless task complexity genuinely demands decomposition, extending Master Vision §3.1's restraint bias into system design.
**Business Importance:** Multi-agent systems multiply failure surfaces, debugging difficulty, and cost; an unjustified architecture choice here compounds across every product built on it.
**AI Importance:** The default every subsequent chapter in Part II must argue against, not assume, when proposing added structural complexity.
**Ethical Importance:** Unnecessary architectural complexity makes a system's behavior harder to audit, which directly undermines the explainability commitments of Ch. 6.
**Dependencies:** Ch. 1, Ch. 7. **Future Dependencies:** Revisit as orchestration frameworks mature and the cost of multi-agent coordination drops relative to today.
**Cross-References:** MV §3.1; PIC Ch. 2 (Product Architecture Philosophy, structural parallel).
**Estimated Page Count:** 5–6 pg. **Suggested Writing Order:** #12. **Complexity Level:** Medium.
**Review Requirements:** AI Review Board; Engineering (PIC owner).
**Success Criteria:** A proposal to add a second agent to an existing single-agent system must justify itself against this chapter's stated bar before architectural work begins.

### 13. Single-Agent System Design
**Purpose:** Define the canonical shape of a single, well-scoped AI agent at Trady Perch — its instruction boundary, its tool access, and the single clear objective it is accountable for.

# RECOMMENDED AUTHORING SEQUENCE

The Suggested Writing Order field on every chapter above is not simply reading order — several chapters are written out of Part sequence because a later Part's chapter is a prerequisite for an earlier Part's chapter to be written correctly, exactly as the Product Implementation Constitution and UX / Experience Blueprint architectures both establish for their own sequences. Four broad authoring phases, each building on the last:

**Phase 1 — Foundation (Ch. 1–24, plus Ch. 91):** Part I in full, then Part II in full, drafting Ch. 91 (Role-Specific Constitutions: Method) immediately after, before any specific role is attempted — this keeps every later role constitution honest about what it must inherit from the start, rather than retrofitted after the fact.

**Phase 2 — Cognition & Action (Ch. 25–63):** Parts III, IV, V, and VI, in that order — reasoning before instruction, instruction before action, action before memory, mirroring the dependency chain each Part's own opening chapter states explicitly.

**Phase 3 — Protection & Experience (Ch. 64–90):** Part VII (Safety) drafted first and treated as a hard gate — no chapter in Part VIII or Part IX is considered final until it has been checked against every applicable Part VII chapter, not merely cross-referenced to it.

**Phase 4 — Application & Endurance (Ch. 91–118):** Part X's nine role-specific constitutions, each individually reviewed against Ch. 91's method before being considered draft-complete; Part XI drafted with explicit provisional labeling per Ch. 100; Part XII drafted last and revisited first, on the standing cadence its own chapters require, for as long as this Constitution remains in force.
