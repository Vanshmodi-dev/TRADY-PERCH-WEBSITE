# CHAPTER 12 — PHOTOGRAPHY SYSTEM

**Trady Perch Design System Bible · Volume I: Foundations**
*First-canonical-draft.*

**Inherited From:** Master Vision §8.1 (Lighting Philosophy), §8.2 (Photography Philosophy), §8.4 (Camera, Lens & Perspective Language), §8.6 (Composition & Cropping Rules). Design System Bible Chapter 1 (P1, P6, P7), Chapter 3 (color grading targets), Chapter 10, Ma-3 (grain).

---

## 1. INTRODUCTION

Master Vision Chapter 8 gives a photographer or art director the *character* the brand's imagery must have — single-source dramatic lighting, subjects occupying a minority of the frame, longer lenses over wide ones, desaturated grading. What it cannot give, because it was written as brand philosophy rather than a working brief, is a document a photographer with no prior context could actually be handed on a shoot day. That document is this chapter.

This chapter depends on Chapter 3 (every color-grading target references the palette defined there) and Chapter 10 (grain intensity, Ma-3). It is consumed directly by any future photography commission and by Chapter 60 (Print & Physical Collateral), which will need this chapter's brief format for physical materials as well as digital ones.

---

## 2. PHILOSOPHY

The rejected alternative is treating each photography commission as its own creative brief, re-derived from Master Vision Chapter 8's prose each time by whoever happens to be running that particular shoot. This produces exactly the drift Principle 7 warns against — two photographers, each reading the same brand prose carefully and in good faith, will not independently arrive at identical lighting or framing decisions. A fixed brief format, specifying the same checklist every time, is what makes photography commissioned a year apart, by different people, still look like it came from the same studio.

---

## 3. CORE PRINCIPLES

### Ph-1 — One Light Source, Named Per Shot

**Purpose.** Every photograph is lit by one dominant, directional light source, stated explicitly in the shot's brief before the shoot begins.

**Reasoning.** Direct restatement of Master Vision §8.1: light that "sculpts rather than reveals" requires a single, intentional source — a brief that doesn't name one in advance risks defaulting to flat, even studio lighting on the day, which §8.1 identifies as reading immediately as generic stock photography.

**Examples.** "Single hard key light, upper-left, 45°, no fill" is a valid stated source. "Well-lit" is not — it names no direction and permits flat lighting by omission.

**When it applies.** To every photograph the brand commissions.

**When it does not apply.** No exception — even a seemingly simple product shot needs a named source, per Principle 1's traceability standard applied to a creative decision.

**Common misunderstandings.** Assuming "one light source" forbids any fill or bounce light entirely. It forbids a second *dominant* source competing with the first — a subtle fill that doesn't compete for attention is compatible with this principle; a second key light of comparable intensity is not.

### Ph-2 — The Subject Occupies 20–35% of Frame Area

**Purpose.** The photographic subject — a person, an object, a piece of hardware — occupies between 20% and 35% of the total frame area, with the remainder treated as intentional negative space, per Master Vision §8.6.

**Reasoning.** Direct numeric resolution of §8.2's "subjects should occupy a minority of the frame" and §3.2's generous negative space principle, applied to photography specifically.

**Examples.** A portrait for a future About page: subject centered or off-center per Chapter 7's asymmetric composition preference, occupying roughly a quarter of the frame, surrounded by deliberate dark negative space rather than cropped tight to fill it.

**When it applies.** To every commissioned photograph.

**When it does not apply.** To a deliberately close macro or texture detail shot (a material study, for Chapter 10's own reference imagery) where the entire frame *is* the subject by design — this is a distinct photographic mode, not a violation of the rule.

**Common misunderstandings.** Assuming a smaller subject-to-frame ratio is always better. Below roughly 15%, a subject risks reading as accidentally small rather than intentionally spacious — the 20–35% range is a deliberately bounded target, not "as small as possible."

### Ph-3 — Lens Compression Over Wide-Angle Distortion

**Purpose.** Photography is shot at focal lengths equivalent to 85mm–135mm on a full-frame sensor. Wide-angle focal lengths (below roughly 35mm equivalent) are not used.

**Reasoning.** Direct resolution of Master Vision §8.4's preference for "the optical characteristics of a longer lens" — flatter perspective, minimal distortion, elegant compression — over the exaggerated depth and edge distortion associated with wide, action-photography, or budget-smartphone lens characteristics.

**Examples.** A workspace texture shot: 100mm equivalent, shallow depth of field, background softly compressed rather than exaggeratedly receding.

**When it applies.** To every commissioned photograph.

**When it does not apply.** To architectural or environmental establishing shots where a wider field of view is functionally necessary to capture the intended space — even here, the widest acceptable equivalent remains bounded (no less than roughly 35mm) rather than defaulting to an ultra-wide lens for dramatic effect.

**Common misunderstandings.** Assuming "longer lens" means "zoomed in tight on a small subject." Focal length and framing are independent — a longer lens can still capture a spacious, 20–35%-subject-ratio frame (Ph-2) simply by increasing camera-to-subject distance.

### Ph-4 — Every Brief Uses One Fixed Checklist

**Purpose.** Every photography brief this brand issues includes, at minimum, five fixed fields: named light source (Ph-1), target subject-to-frame ratio (Ph-2), focal length (Ph-3), color-grade target (Section 4), and grain specification (Chapter 10, Ma-3).

**Reasoning.** Descends from Principle 7 applied to the brief document itself: a checklist that is sometimes complete and sometimes abbreviated, depending on who wrote it, reintroduces exactly the drift this chapter exists to prevent.

**Examples.** No photography commission proceeds to shoot day without all five fields completed in the brief, regardless of how experienced the photographer or how "obvious" the brand's style may seem to them already.

**When it applies.** To every photography commission, without exception.

**When it does not apply.** No exception.

**Common misunderstandings.** Treating the checklist as a formality to complete quickly rather than a genuine specification tool. A brief that fills in all five fields with vague values ("some light," "medium distance") satisfies the checklist's letter while failing its purpose — each field must carry the actual specificity this chapter's other principles require.

---

## 4. COMPLETE DESIGN SPECIFICATION

**Fixed brief checklist (Ph-4), with defaults:**

1. **Light source:** single, directional, named by position and hardness (e.g., "hard key, 45° upper-left, no fill" or "soft key, near-horizontal, gentle falloff").
2. **Subject-to-frame ratio:** 20–35% (Ph-2), stated as a specific target within that range per shot.
3. **Focal length:** 85–135mm full-frame equivalent (Ph-3), stated exactly.
4. **Color-grade target:** desaturated, cool-to-neutral overall grade; any gold present in-frame must originate from an actual light source or reflective surface (Chapter 10, Ma-4), never added as a color-grade filter over an otherwise neutral photograph, per Master Vision §8.2.
5. **Grain:** Chapter 10's fixed system-wide grain intensity, applied as a final post-process step.

**Forbidden content**, restated from Master Vision §6.4 for enforceability here: no generic "diverse team laughing at a laptop" staging, no direct-eye-contact corporate headshot posing, no visible third-party logos in frame.

---

## 5. MEASUREMENTS

- **Subject-to-frame ratio:** 20–35%.
- **Focal length range:** 85–135mm (full-frame equivalent); absolute minimum for establishing shots: 35mm.
- **Checklist fields:** 5, all required, per Ph-4.

---

## 6. BEHAVIORAL RULES

**Before any shoot.** Complete all five checklist fields (Ph-4) and confirm the light source is genuinely singular and dominant (Ph-1).

**During review of delivered assets.** Verify subject ratio (Ph-2) and check for any color grading applied that doesn't originate from an in-frame light source (Ph-1, Chapter 10 Ma-4).

---

## 7. MOTION SPECIFICATION

Not directly applicable — photography is a static medium. Where a photograph is used within a motion context (a Ken Burns-style slow pan or zoom on a hero background), that motion uses Chapter 15's Deliberate tier at most, never Quick or Instant, since a moving photograph in this system functions as an ambient, contemplative background element, not an interactive response.

---

## 8. ACCESSIBILITY

Every photograph used in a meaningful (non-purely-decorative) context requires descriptive alternative text sufficient for a screen-reader user to understand its informational content, per Master Vision §22 — purely atmospheric background imagery with no informational content may use empty/null alternative text, but this determination must be made deliberately per image, not defaulted to null out of convenience.

---

## 9. RESPONSIVE BEHAVIOUR

Images are art-directed per breakpoint where the 20–35% subject ratio (Ph-2) would otherwise be violated by a naive crop — a wide, generously-spaced desktop photograph cropped straight to a narrow mobile viewport will often push the subject ratio outside the specified range, and a separate, deliberately recomposed mobile crop should be produced rather than accepting whatever a center-crop algorithm produces by default.

---

## 10. AI & FUTURE INTERFACES

A spatial environment (Chapter 73) would present photography as a flat plane within a three-dimensional space rather than replace the photograph itself — this chapter's lighting and composition rules remain the governing standard for the image asset; only its placement and framing within the spatial scene is Chapter 73's separate concern.

---

## 11. DO

Commissioning a workspace texture photograph with a stated single hard key light from the upper-left, subject (a piece of desk hardware) at roughly 25% of frame, shot at 100mm equivalent, graded cool and desaturated with grain applied per Chapter 10 — a complete, specific brief satisfying all five checklist fields.

## 12. DON'T

Approving a delivered photograph because it "feels on-brand" without checking it against the five-field checklist. A photograph can feel broadly appropriate while still using flat, multi-source lighting or a wide, distorted lens that a careful review against Ph-1 and Ph-3 would catch immediately.

---

## 13. ANTI-PATTERNS

**Brief-by-vibe.** Commissioning photography with only a mood board or a general style reference ("something like the Master Vision's description") rather than this chapter's specific five-field checklist. This is dangerous because a mood board communicates a feeling, not a specification, and different photographers will translate the same feeling into meaningfully different lighting, framing, and lens choices. It is detected by checking whether a commissioned shoot's brief document actually contains all five required fields with specific values, not general references. It is fixed by completing the checklist before the shoot, not after reviewing the results.

---

## 14. QUALITY ASSURANCE CHECKLIST

- [ ] Is the light source single, directional, and named specifically? *(Ph-1)*
- [ ] Does the subject occupy 20–35% of the frame? *(Ph-2)*
- [ ] Is the focal length within the 85–135mm range (or no less than 35mm for establishing shots)? *(Ph-3)*
- [ ] Does the brief include all five required fields, each with specific, non-vague values? *(Ph-4)*
- [ ] Is any gold visible in-frame sourced from real light/reflection, never an added color-grade filter?
- [ ] Does the image avoid every forbidden content category from Section 4?
