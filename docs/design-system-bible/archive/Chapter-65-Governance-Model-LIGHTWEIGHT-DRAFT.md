# CHAPTER 65 — GOVERNANCE MODEL & DECISION RIGHTS
## (Lightweight Phase 1 Draft)

**Trady Perch Design System Bible · Volume VI: Quality, Governance & Evolution**

**Status: intentionally incomplete.** Per the Architecture's Recommended Authoring Sequence, this chapter exists twice: a lightweight draft now, in Phase 1, because Chapters 2 through 63 already needed *somewhere* to point token and naming disagreements toward — and a full, retrospective version later, in Phase 6, once real disagreements have actually occurred to learn from. This draft answers only what Phase 1–3 chapters need answered. Nothing here is invalidated when the full version is written; per Gov-3 below, it is superseded, not retroactively wrong.

**Inherited From:** Design System Bible Chapter 1 (P1, P8), Chapter 2, §4 (which named the interim authority this chapter formalizes).

---

## 1. INTRODUCTION

Chapter 2 needed an approval authority for token proposals before this chapter existed to provide one properly, and named a placeholder — the Design System Architect role — to hold that authority provisionally. This draft exists to make that placeholder real enough to actually function, without attempting the full governance model Chapter 66 (Component Lifecycle) and Chapter 67 (Contribution Guidelines) will eventually need once real components, not just tokens, are in play.

This draft depends on Chapter 1's Principle 8 (The Impossible Standard as Final Arbiter) directly — it is the escalation path P8 requires to exist. It is depended on by every chapter written since Chapter 2 that references "the interim authority" or "escalation" without further detail.

---

## 2. PHILOSOPHY

The alternative rejected here is deferring any governance answer at all until Phase 6, leaving Phase 1–3 chapters to reference an authority that does not yet exist. This was rejected because several already-written chapters (Chapter 2, Section 4; Chapter 63) explicitly point to this chapter for approval and escalation — an unresolved forward reference in a document that otherwise insists nothing be left to interpretation would be a direct contradiction of this Bible's own standard. A minimal, honest, clearly-labeled-as-provisional answer is preferable to either silence or a fully engineered governance system built before any real disagreement has tested it.

---

## 3. CORE PRINCIPLES

### Gov-1 — One Named Authority Until Governance Is Formalized

**Purpose.** Until this chapter's full version exists, a single named role — the Design System Architect — holds approval authority for token, naming, and Foundations-chapter proposals.

**Reasoning.** Descends from Principle 1: an authority that isn't named is an authority nobody can actually cite, which defeats the purpose of naming one at all.

**When it applies.** To any proposal made under Chapters 1–16 or 63 before Chapter 65's full version supersedes this draft.

**When it does not apply.** To decisions this Bible's principles (Chapter 1) already resolve without needing escalation — most proposals should never reach this authority at all, per Chapter 1's own note that most apparent conflicts dissolve before reaching final arbitration.

### Gov-2 — Escalation Requires a Documented Disagreement

**Purpose.** The interim authority is invoked only once two contributors have documented an actual, specific disagreement about whether a proposal satisfies a named principle — never as a first step, and never for a matter of undocumented personal preference.

**Reasoning.** Descends from Chapter 1's derivation test (Section 4): the test's first four steps are designed to resolve most questions without escalation; this principle keeps this chapter's lightweight authority from becoming a shortcut around that test.

**When it applies.** To any Phase 1–3 disagreement that survives the derivation test's first four steps.

**When it does not apply.** To questions the derivation test itself already resolves.

### Gov-3 — No Retroactive Authority

**Purpose.** A decision approved under this lightweight draft remains valid once Chapter 65's full version is written, unless specifically revisited through that chapter's own process.

**Reasoning.** Descends from Chapter 2's T-5 (tokens are deprecated, never silently deleted) applied to governance decisions themselves: a full governance system that retroactively invalidates everything decided before it existed would punish the exact early chapters this draft was written to unblock.

**When it applies.** To every decision made under Gov-1/Gov-2 before Chapter 65's full version ships.

**When it does not apply.** To a decision independently found to violate a Chapter 1 principle, which was always invalid regardless of who approved it.

## 4. COMPLETE DESIGN SPECIFICATION

The Design System Architect role, for the duration of this draft's validity, holds sole approval authority for: new Core-tier tokens (Chapter 2), naming convention exceptions (Chapter 63), and any Foundations-chapter (1–16) proposal escalated per Gov-2. This authority does not extend to Volume II component decisions, which await Chapter 65's full version and Chapter 17's Anatomy Standard being in place first.

## 6. BEHAVIORAL RULES

**Before escalating.** Confirm the derivation test's first four steps (Chapter 1, Section 4) have been run and did not resolve the disagreement.

**Under escalation.** The Design System Architect's decision is documented with the same rigor Chapter 1, Section 4, Step 5 requires — reasoning recorded, not just a verdict.

## 11. DO

Escalating a genuine disagreement about whether a proposed Core color value duplicates an existing one (Chapter 3, C-1) after the derivation test failed to resolve it independently.

## 12. DON'T

Escalating a matter of personal taste ("I'd prefer a slightly warmer gold") without a stated principle-level basis — this is not a disagreement Gov-2 recognizes as escalation-worthy.

---

## 13. ANTI-PATTERNS

**Authority shopping.** Repeatedly escalating a rejected proposal in slightly reworded form, hoping for a different outcome. This is dangerous because it treats the interim authority as an obstacle to route around rather than a genuine check — it is detected by tracking repeated proposals on the same underlying question, and fixed by requiring any re-proposal to state what new information changed since the prior rejection.

---

## 14. QUALITY ASSURANCE CHECKLIST

- [ ] Has the derivation test been run and genuinely failed to resolve the question before escalating? *(Gov-2)*
- [ ] Is the disagreement being escalated to the named interim authority, not resolved informally by seniority or persistence? *(Gov-1)*
- [ ] Is the decision documented with its reasoning, not only its verdict?
