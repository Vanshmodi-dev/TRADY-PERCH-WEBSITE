# TRADY PERCH — HERO DIRECTION: "FIELD LINES"

**Design direction. Supersedes the Variation B recommendation in [Hero Concept Proposals](Trady-Perch-Hero-Concept-Proposals.md) Part Two.**

**Governing objective:** not architectural purity. One of the most memorable premium hero experiences on the internet — optimised for emotional impact, cinematic storytelling, and brand memorability.

---

## 1. THE CENTRAL IDEA

**The wordmark is not assembled. It is magnetised into existence.**

This is the whole direction in one sentence, and it is what separates this from every particle hero that already exists.

Conventional particle logo formation is *additive assembly*: dots travel in straight lines to predetermined positions and stop. It reads as software executing a script, which is why it has become generic — it looks the same regardless of what the company does.

**Field Lines uses a different physical model.** An invisible magnetic field exists in the volume. Gold metallic dust is suspended in it. When the field energises, the dust does what iron filings on a sheet of paper do above a magnet: it **rotates to align with the field first**, then **migrates along curved field lines** toward the poles. The wordmark is the field's shape. The particles do not build it — they *reveal* it.

Three things follow, and each one is a brand argument:

**It reads as engineering, not magic.** Iron filings in a magnetic field is one of the most recognisable demonstrations in physics, and it is unmistakably *instrumentation*. Nobody watching this thinks "particle library." They think "someone modelled a field."

**It preserves the strongest idea from The Instrument.** *The system was already there; you are watching it become visible.* The field exists before the dust reveals it. We did not trade the good idea for spectacle — we dramatised it.

**It is category-differentiating within the particle space.** Every competitor doing particles is doing assembly. Alignment-then-migration is a completely different motion signature, and it is visibly harder to fake.

## 2. THE SEQUENCE

**Total: 5,000ms.** Skippable at any moment. Once per session.

Those two constraints are kept not for governance reasons but because they *protect the effect*: an unskippable five-second intro on the third visit converts the most memorable moment on the site into the most irritating one.

### Beat 0 — BLACK · 400ms
Not empty. A barely perceptible grain, and one dim gold point far off-centre — a distant filament, almost subliminal. Silence. Something is present before it is visible.

### Beat 1 — THE CHARGE · 500ms
The point brightens almost imperceptibly. Around it, gold dust becomes faintly visible — not *appearing*, but being *revealed* by growing light. The dust is suspended, motionless, randomly distributed and randomly oriented. This is unordered matter in the dark.

### Beat 2 — FIELD IGNITION · 700ms
**The signature shot.** The field energises. Every particle **rotates to align with the local field direction** — no translation yet, pure alignment. Thousands of elongated metallic flecks turning in unison, each catching the key light differently as it turns, so the entire volume *shimmers* from noise into ordered texture.

This is the beat nobody has done, and it is where the "holy shit" lives. It is invisible forces made visible — which is a literal description of what this company sells.

### Beat 3 — MIGRATION · 1,200ms
Particles move along **curved field lines**, not straight paths, flowing toward the poles. Density concentrates where the letterforms will be.

The letters do not appear — **density** appears. The wordmark is perceived as an emerging concentration several hundred milliseconds before it becomes legible. That perceptual gap is the most cinematic part of the sequence.

*Craft-critical:* migration is asynchronous and unhurried. A long ease-out tail; stragglers still drifting in as the form becomes readable. Simultaneous arrival reads as flashy. Staggered arrival reads as physical.

### Beat 4 — SETTLE · 400ms
"TRADY PERCH" resolves to full legibility. Particles that overshot snap back a fraction — one brief magnetic seat, the visual equivalent of a mechanism clicking home. Then stillness.

### Beat 5 — THE SWEEP · 600ms
A hard raking light crosses the formed wordmark. Its job is to confirm a **state change**: the dust has become *solid metal*. The sweep is the proof, and it is the moment the wordmark stops being particles and starts being an object.

Five states of matter in five seconds: suspended dust → aligned dust → concentrated dust → form → material.

### Beat 6 — THE TAGLINE · 400ms
**BUILD. AUTOMATE. GROW.** sets beneath in widened caps tracking. **No particle effect.** It simply *is*.

The contrast is deliberate and it is the sequence's quietest good idea: after all that formation, one line that never needed to form. The brand performs once, then speaks plainly.

### Beat 7 — THE PULL-BACK · 800ms
The camera pulls back — and we discover the wordmark was **etched into the surface of a machined object all along.** What we took for a title card was a macro detail shot.

The remaining suspended dust settles onto the object's surface and into the light shaft around it.

**This is the real reveal, and it is a brand statement, not an effect:** *there is more here than you thought, and it was there the whole time.* For an agency selling depth of capability, that is precisely on-message in a way that a logo animation never is.

### Beat 8 — HERO REST
The object sits in frame. Claim, qualifier and CTA resolve into the light. The dust is now static — settled on the surface, suspended in the beam.

## 3. THE PARTICLE SPECIFICATION — WHY IT READS AS METAL, NOT GLITTER

This section is the difference between premium and cheap, and every item is a physical-believability lever.

| Property | Specification | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Form** | Elongated flecks with an aspect ratio, not spheres | Spheres read as glitter. Elongated + oriented reads as filings. **The single most important decision here.** |
| **Emission** | **Zero.** Particles never glow | Glitter glows; metal reflects. Every unit of brightness must come from the key light |
| **Specular** | Anisotropic — directional highlights, as brushed metal has | Particles *flash* as they rotate through the light's angle. This is what sells "metallic" |
| **Orientation** | Every particle has a rotation state driven by the field | Alignment is the entire conceit; without per-particle orientation there is no field |
| **Depth of field** | Near particles genuinely out of focus | Instantly reads as photographed rather than rendered |
| **Motion blur** | On fast-moving particles during migration only | Removes the "sprite" quality that marks cheap systems |
| **Participation** | ~70% form the wordmark; ~30% remain suspended | The leftovers become the hero's ambient dust — **the same dust**, which makes the transition continuous rather than staged |
| **Density** | Restrained. Legibility should arrive from concentration, not volume | Too many particles reads as a fog and destroys the letterform |
| **Colour** | Gold as *reflected* warm light on metal, never as a tint applied to a sprite | Gold must be a material property, not a colour choice |

## 4. THE 3D OBJECT — "THE CORE"

**What it is.** A machined artefact, roughly palm-sized in implied scale: a matte-black anodised housing with a precisely cut aperture, through which brushed metal internals are visible — and inside those, a **slowly rotating gold element**. The reference space is a mechanical watch movement crossed with a server component designed by someone who makes watches.

**Why this object and not another.** It is an *engine* — the thing that runs quietly and continuously in the background, which is literally what this company builds and installs. It is not a robot, a brain, a network graph, or an abstract blob. It is a component, and no competitor in this category has one.

**The brand idea embedded in its construction:** the housing is matte black and unremarkable; the gold is *inside*. **The value is in the system, not on its surface.** That is a genuine brand statement expressed as an object, and it gives gold both an honest source and a reason to exist.

**Continuous motion.** The object rotates on its own axis extremely slowly — on the order of one revolution per minute, at the threshold of perception. It should feel like a display piece on a turntable in a vitrine, not like an animation.

**Mouse interaction — light, not rotation.** Pointer movement does **not** turn the object to face the cursor. Pointer movement **moves the key light** across it.

This is a better interaction model for four reasons: it is physically believable (you are moving a light source, not magically rotating an object); it rewards exploration by *revealing material* — which is the entire point of a machined surface; it cannot look gimmicky, because light response is what real objects do; and it makes the volumetric dust respond too, so the whole *room* reacts rather than just the object.

**Damping is the luxury.** The light follows the pointer with heavy easing — a long, weighted lag with a generous ease-out tail. Instant tracking feels twitchy and cheap. Weighted tracking feels like moving a heavy lamp. This single parameter is the difference between "premium" and "interactive demo."

---

## 5. LIGHTING — PHYSICALLY BELIEVABLE

A four-light rig, all motivated:

**Key.** Hard, narrow, from upper-left at roughly 25°. **Volumetric** — visible as a shaft in the suspended dust. This is the light the pointer moves, and it is what reveals both the object's machining and the particles' orientation.

**Fill.** Very dim, cool, opposite side, roughly 1:8 — deliberately more dramatic than a product-shot ratio. Shadow is retained, not lifted. Deep black is where the object's mass comes from.

**Practical — the gold emitter.** The rotating element *inside* the object emits warm light through the aperture. This is the only warm source in the scene and it is fully motivated: **gold light comes from inside the machine.** It is not applied to surfaces; it escapes from the working part.

That is the strongest single image in this direction. It resolves the brand's gold discipline better than any surface treatment could — gold is not an accent, it is what the mechanism produces.

**Rim.** A subtle cool kicker from behind, separating the object from the black field so it does not muddy into the background. Standard cinematography, invisible when done correctly, badly missed when absent.

## 6. TYPOGRAPHY DIRECTION

**The wordmark** must be drawn to survive particle formation: generous counters, consistent stroke weight, geometric sans, light-to-regular. Forms with tight apertures collapse into mush at particle density. This constrains the mark's construction and should be settled before any sequence work begins.

**The tagline** sets in widened caps tracking beneath, arriving whole.

**The hero claim** is **not** particle-formed. That device has been spent, and re-using it would cheapen both instances. The claim arrives simply — light weight, large, left-aligned, sitting within the lit region. Four sizes total in the resting viewport.

The rhythm across the whole experience: **the brand performs once, then speaks plainly.** Spectacle, then substance. A hero that kept performing would read as a company that cannot stop selling.

## 7. WHY THIS IS STRONGER FROM A BRANDING PERSPECTIVE

**1. Memorability is a commercial asset that credibility alone does not buy.** The prior recommendation optimised for credibility-per-second and never priced recall. But this business runs on referral — *"you should look at these guys"* — and referral requires something transmissible. A hero with no peak is optimally credible and forgettable. Every named reference brand has a signature moment.

**2. The magnetic conceit differentiates inside the particle category.** The objection to particles was that they are generic. That is true of *assembly*. Field alignment is a different phenomenon with a different motion signature, and it reads as physics rather than as a plugin. This is the difference between using a particle library and modelling a field.

**3. It keeps the strongest brand idea rather than trading it away.** *The system was already there; you are seeing it become visible.* The field precedes the dust. The concept survived the redesign and got more vivid.

**4. The pull-back is the brand statement.** *There is more here than you thought, and it was there all along.* That is what an agency selling depth of capability needs a stranger to feel, and it is delivered structurally rather than claimed in copy.

**5. Gold from inside the machine is a brand-defining image.** It is ownable, it is physically honest, and it says the value is internal — which is exactly what "we build the systems that run underneath your business" means, expressed without a word.

**6. The object gives the brand a recurring asset.** A machined Core can appear in case-study imagery, the deck, social, and print. A particle animation cannot leave the homepage. This direction produces an identity object, not just an intro.

## 8. WHAT PROTECTS THE EFFECT

Framed as engineering that serves the goal, because that is what these are — a stuttering particle system is far worse for a "holy shit" reaction than no particle system at all.

- **Frame rate is the whole ballgame.** Any judder converts premium into amateur instantly and irreversibly. Particle count should be tuned down until it holds on mid-range hardware, then tuned no further up. Fewer, better-lit, correctly-oriented particles beat more particles every time.
- **The claim must exist underneath from the first frame.** Server-rendered, present in the DOM before the sequence runs. This costs nothing visually and means a skip, a slow connection, or a JS failure reveals a finished hero rather than an empty one.
- **Skippable at any moment; once per session.** Both protect the moment rather than dilute it.
- **Reduced motion needs a designed still, not a deletion** — and a good one exists: the Core, lit, wordmark etched, dust settled in the beam. That single frame is genuinely strong and should be authored deliberately.
- **Mobile needs re-composition, not scaling.** The pull-back's framing and the particle density both have to be redesigned for a vertical viewport; a scaled desktop sequence will read as cramped.
