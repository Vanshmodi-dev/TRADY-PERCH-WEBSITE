# ADR-0008: The homepage intro sequence's LCP cost, accepted rather than shortened

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-07-25
**Origin:** Master Vision §9.2 (Intro Experience); Product Implementation Constitution Ch.35 §3–§4 (Performance Philosophy — non-negotiable-floor authority, content-over-animation priority), Ch.36 §2 (Performance Budgets — Marketing Site LCP ≤ 2000ms); Design System Bible Chapter 55 Pf-2 (see also [[0009-pf2-reduced-motion-as-sole-degradation-signal]] — a related but distinct gap: this ADR is about *timing* the same sequence trades against, that one is about *simplifying* it for weak hardware; both land on the same "disclose, don't game the metric" resolution).

## Context

Milestone 8's real-browser measurement (`apps/marketing-site/scripts/performance-audit.mjs`, cross-checked with a Lighthouse phase-breakdown diagnostic) found the homepage's Largest Contentful Paint element is the intro sequence's own logo mark image (`IntroSequence`, `intro-sequence.tsx`), and its LCP measured ~3950–4260ms against Ch.36 §2's 2000ms budget — the worst of any route by a wide margin.

The first-pass fix was a genuine defect, not this tension: `IntroSequence` previously kept its entire markup — including the `<Image priority>` that turns out to be the LCP element — out of the DOM until a client-side `sessionStorage` check resolved (a `setTimeout(0)` past hydration), so the browser had no way to even discover and start fetching the image until then. Fixed by rendering the markup unconditionally with a CSS-only `.hidden` state (`visibility: hidden`) standing in for the old `return null`, so the image (and `next/image`'s own `priority`-driven `<link rel="preload">`) exist in the very first server-rendered HTML. Verified via the same phase-breakdown diagnostic: this took the LCP's "Load Delay" phase from 1424ms (42% of the total) to 0ms — a real, confirmed win, and correct regardless of anything below.

That fix alone did not close the budget gap. The diagnostic's phase breakdown after the fix: TTFB ~467ms, Load Time ~450ms, **Render Delay ~3037ms (77% of the total)**. Render Delay is the time between the image finishing its download and actually being *painted* — and that's gated by `IntroSequence`'s own phase state machine, not by resource loading: the mark's `opacity: 0 → 1` reveal is held behind `markVisible = phase !== "silence"`, and `phase` only leaves `"silence"` after Master Vision §9.2's own deliberate opening beat (`TIMINGS.silence = 400ms`) plus the async session-check overhead ahead of it, all under Lighthouse's simulated mobile-CPU throttling, which inflates the real-time cost of every state update and re-render along that chain.

In short: the remaining ~3s+ of LCP time is the ceremonial sequence's own intentional pacing, not a fixable engineering delay. Shortening `TIMINGS.silence` or fast-forwarding `markVisible` to game this specific metric would mean editing the one thing Master Vision §9.2 states most specifically ("a controlled, adult version of delight," a first-visit-only brand moment with named stages) purely to move a number — exactly the trade Ch.35 §4 itself warns against making carelessly, just pointed in the opposite direction from its own stated example (it warns against sacrificing *content* for animation; this would be sacrificing a *deliberate brand moment* for a metric, on a page where the real content — Hero's headline — is already server-rendered and available underneath, just not what a lab tool happens to measure as "largest").

## Decision

Keep the ceremonial intro sequence's pacing exactly as designed. Ship the Load-Delay fix (real, verified, no tradeoff). Accept, explicitly and on the record, that the homepage's LCP will not clear Ch.36 §2's 2000ms budget for a first-time visitor during the one-time intro, and log this as an open Ch.66 debt-register entry rather than silently accepting a failing automated check or unilaterally cutting the intro's timing to force a pass.

This is scoped narrowly: every other route (28 of 29 measured) has no intro sequence and is evaluated against the same 2000ms budget on its own merits, unaffected by this decision. A **returning** visitor within the same session also never re-triggers the sequence visually (per Master Vision §9.2's own "never twice in one session" rule) — though, as an honest side-effect of the Load-Delay fix itself, `next/image`'s `priority`-driven preload link for `/logo-mark.jpeg` is now emitted on every homepage load regardless of `skippedThisSession`, since the markup renders unconditionally. This is a genuinely new, small, extra fetch for a returning visitor that didn't happen before (the sitewide header/footer icon references a separate, much smaller `/logo-mark-icon.webp` variant as of the same milestone's review — see that finding — so there's no cache overlap between the two). At ~13KB for a same-session-only, already-visited-page repeat load, this is judged immaterial and not worth undoing the Load-Delay fix over; noted here rather than left as an unqualified "unaffected."

## Consequences

- `apps/marketing-site/scripts/performance-audit.mjs` will continue to report the homepage's LCP as a budget violation until either this ADR is revisited or the ceremonial sequence's design changes — this is expected, not a bug in the audit script, and should not be "fixed" by loosening the script's threshold or excluding the homepage from measurement.
- The Ch.66 debt-register entry for this is the record of record; anyone re-litigating the homepage's LCP number should start there, not re-diagnose from scratch.
- If Master Vision §9.2 itself is ever revised (a business/brand decision, not an engineering one), this ADR should be revisited alongside it — the LCP budget was never the reason the ceremonial pacing exists, and shouldn't become the reason it changes either.

## Correction (Milestone 10, 2026-07-25)

This ADR's "scoped narrowly... every other route... unaffected by this decision" claim (Decision section, above) is **wrong** and is corrected here rather than silently edited away, per this Constitution's own Chapter 68 Facet 2 (removing an honest correction to look more finished is worse than leaving the original mistake visible alongside its fix).

Milestone 10's final-QA run of `performance-audit.mjs` across all 29 routes found **every route**, not only the homepage, reports an LCP over Ch.36 §2's 2000ms budget under Lighthouse (3038–4239ms). The homepage is still the worst (its own ceremonial-pacing cost is additive on top of the shared cause below), but the other 28 routes — which have no intro sequence at all — are not "unaffected" as this ADR originally claimed.

Root cause, confirmed two ways:
1. Per-route JS bytes are 0.0KB on every route (see the same audit run's bundle-size table) — 100% of every route's JS is the ~165KB shared/vendor bundle already logged as over its own 150KB ceiling in Chapter 66. Lighthouse's default Node-API config applies simulated mobile CPU throttling (4x slowdown); parsing and hydrating a 165KB bundle under that throttling is genuinely slow enough to delay paint by seconds, on every route, independent of that route's own content.
2. A direct, unthrottled real-browser measurement (`scripts/lcp-check.mjs`, a one-off diagnostic written for this correction, real `PerformanceObserver` LCP entries against a real production server, no Lighthouse involved) found `/` at 276ms, `/legal` at 208ms, `/pricing` at 220ms, `/solutions/ai-agents` at 228ms — all excellent, confirming this is a lab-throttling artifact of the shared bundle's size, not a real-world user-facing regression, and not specific to the homepage's ceremonial sequence at all.

This does not change this ADR's actual Decision (the ceremonial intro's pacing is still kept, for the same Master Vision §9.2 reasons) — it changes the *scope claim* around it. The corresponding Chapter 66 entry has been rewritten to match; see it for the corrected, full-scope record.
