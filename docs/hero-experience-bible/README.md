## THE ARGUMENT IN ONE PAGE

**The mission** (Ch. 1): the hero exists to convert a stranger's reflex judgment into a considered decision to keep reading — by demonstrating, in fifteen seconds and without any claim being believed, that this company is competent, composed, and worth a skeptical person's time.

**The bet** (Ch. 2): in a category saturated with claims, the most credible available signal is the visible decision not to make one. Restraint is not the absence of persuasion; it is persuasion by demonstrated indifference to the outcome of any single visit — which only works if the underlying calm is real.

**The mechanism** (Ch. 3–8): personality is absorbed before content is read; the visitor's processing budget is roughly four chunks; they arrive guarded rather than neutral, so the hero's first job is to *lower* resistance rather than raise interest; attention is captured involuntarily, so the most important thing must also be the most salient; and the hero is the first sentence of a fifteen-section argument, not its synopsis.

**The identity problem** (Ch. 9–12): every visual convention for signalling AI capability now signals its absence, so the hero cannot depict competence — it must *be* competent, and say nothing about it. Meanwhile it must read as neither a freelancer with a template nor a consultancy that will bill for eighteen months.

**The structure** (Ch. 13–18): one hero serves five visitor intents without adapting to any; five goals in strict lexicographic order with six named anti-goals; three hierarchy rungs (what changes → for whom → what now); persuasion by risk-reduction rather than pressure; trust built almost entirely by removing counter-signals; and copy judged on specificity above every other property.

**The language** (Ch. 19–24): scale without weight; space as the only unrationed budget; a motion budget already spent on the one ceremonial moment; 3D that must earn its place against a flat alternative; one light source; three materials.

**The behaviour** (Ch. 25–27): immediate acknowledgement, nothing decorative responding, the system cursor left alone, and exactly one honestly-priced action.

**The obligations** (Ch. 28–29): accessibility as evidence rather than compliance, and performance as a capability disclosure — with one narrow, documented, accepted budget violation that protects the ceremonial intro and nothing else.

**The endurance** (Ch. 30–34): the same hero in two contexts whose psychology differs more than their dimensions; a governance model that assumes continuous one-directional pressure toward addition; twenty-two named failures; and one hundred checks, twenty-three of them fatal.

## HONEST STATUS NOTES

Consistent with this Bible's own standard against false completeness (Ch. 17 Tb-5, Ch. 32 Fx-5), several things are worth stating plainly rather than implying otherwise:

1. **No hero has been built under this Bible.** Every principle here is reasoned from the source documents and from established psychology; none has been validated against real visitors, real analytics, or real revenue. Appendix A §3.3 lists the ten claims most likely to be wrong, including the phase boundaries in Chapter 6 and the checklist thresholds in Chapter 34.

2. **Chapter 33's anti-pattern catalogue is predictive, not incident-derived.** It has no production failures behind it — the same honest status the Design System Bible's Chapter 68 and the Constitution's Chapter 67 record for themselves. Entries that never occur should be removed; failures observed but unlisted should be added.

3. **The absence of real proof content is load-bearing, and it weakens one argument here.** Chapter 10 §9 states it directly: the hero's restraint is meant to signal *"we can afford not to do this,"* but with no case studies, client names, or metrics anywhere on the site yet, an equally consistent reading is *"they have nothing to show."* Both readings produce the same hero; the visitor chooses between them based on what is below it. This is the strongest argument in this Bible for prioritising real proof content.

4. **Several chapters defer to sibling chapters that are not written.** The intro sequence's per-beat timing, hero content stagger, social-proof placement, and cognitive-accessibility doctrine all live in unwritten Motion Bible and UX Blueprint chapters. Those gaps are catalogued in Appendix A §3.1 and are deliberately *not* filled here — inventing values would create a conflict the moment the owning chapter is authored.

5. **This Bible has never been amended, so Chapter 32's procedure is untested.** The first real amendment will reveal whether it is too heavy, too light, or routed to the wrong governance body. That finding should be recorded, not quietly fixed.

6. **On length.** This is a condensed edition — roughly a third shorter than the first draft, with the reasoning tightened and cross-chapter repetition removed. Every principle, matrix, diagram, and check from the original survives. It runs longer than the 70–100 page target: at 165 principles, 100 checks, 22 anti-patterns and ~60 decision instruments, the remaining bulk is content rather than prose, and cutting further would mean removing substance instead of redundancy.

Everything else — the mission, the four standing tests, the fifteen-second timeline, the goal stack, the three-rung hierarchy, the trust and conversion doctrines, the sensory language, the universal obligations, and the governance model — is complete, internally consistent, and ready to design against.
