# CHAPTER 17 — TRUST BUILDING

**Trady Perch Hero Experience Bible · Part V: Persuasion & Trust**
*First-canonical-draft. Implementation-independent.*

**Inherited From:** Master Vision §2.3 (the Trust Equation), Ch. 27 item 7, Ch. 28, Ch. 15. UX Blueprint Ch. 3, Ch. 17, Ch. 53.
**Governs:** What trust the hero can build, what it must not attempt, the sequencing constraint on proof, and the events that destroy trust instantly.
**Does Not Govern:** The proof content itself or its placement in later sections.

---

## 1. THE POSITION

**The hero has no proof and cannot acquire any.** No case study, client name, metric, testimonial — and per §14.2, not even a company story. Everything the Trust Equation ranks as decisive lives further down. The hero's contribution is narrow and often underestimated: **it establishes whether the proof further down will be believed when it arrives.**

That framing answers the question this chapter is most often asked — *why not put a client logo in the hero?* — better than any argument about clutter. Proof presented before a claim exists has nothing to attach to. It is not merely wasted; it is *spent*, because a logo strip seen at second four and forgotten by second twelve cannot be shown again at full force at section eight.

**The Trust Equation.** §2.3 ranks what this buyer cares about: (1) evidence of past outcomes, (2) evidence of process rigor, (3) evidence of technical depth, (4) aesthetic polish — explicitly last, "a hygiene factor, not a decision factor."

The hero's honest position: **it can deliver only the fourth directly.** But a *hygiene factor* is one whose absence disqualifies while its presence earns little — the shape of a precondition. So the hero's trust job is threefold: **clear the hygiene bar** so items 1–3 get a fair hearing; **establish source credibility** so later evidence reads as coming from a competent party; and **introduce no counter-signal**, because one inconsistency destroys accumulated trust faster than any signal builds it.

**The sequencing constraint.** Ch. 17: **evidence before claim, specificity before generality, third-party validation before self-assertion.** Read carefully, this constrains the hero more than it enables it. "Evidence before claim" does not mean *put evidence in the hero* — it means a claim must be accompanied by evidence *where it is made*. The hero's resolution is the opposite of what the phrasing suggests: **because it cannot carry evidence, it must make only claims that require none.** A claim requiring no evidence is a description — "we build systems that remove manual work from quoting" is checkable against the sections below and is not a boast. "We are the leading experts in AI transformation" requires evidence the hero cannot supply.

**Trust asymmetry.** Fifty small positive signals move trust a little; one clear negative signal moves it a lot, in the wrong direction, unrecoverably by adding more positive ones. **So the hero's trust strategy is defensive:** its highest-value work is the systematic elimination of counter-signals. This is the same conclusion Chapter 16 reached about persuasion, arrived at independently — reasonable evidence the model is coherent.

## 2. CORE PRINCIPLES

**Tb-1 — Trust is demonstrated, never asserted.** No assertion about the company's trustworthiness, reliability, expertise, or standing. Ch. 28 forbids "trustworthy" in copy at all. An assertion invites evaluation the visitor has no basis to perform, so the default resolution is scepticism. *Description is not assertion.*

**Tb-2 — No claim the hero cannot support.** Every claim is either self-evidently descriptive or substantiated by a section below. An unsupported claim becomes a *discovered inconsistency* the moment a visitor looks for support and fails — triggering the asymmetry above. *The support lives below; the requirement is that it exists.*

**Tb-3 — Proof belongs where it can be verified.** No proof device in the hero: no client logo, metric, testimonial, rating, certification, or funding note. Proof is persuasive in proportion to the visitor's ability to interrogate it. A logo in the hero is an image; the same logo on a case study they can read, attached to a named problem and a measured outcome, is evidence. *Proof is the most important trust content on the site — which is why it is not spent in the hero.*

**Tb-4 — Eliminate counter-signals before adding signals.** A hero with three positive signals and one counter-signal is net negative; the same hero with zero of each is net neutral and materially better. *Not passivity — §4's catalogue is a demanding programme of work, most of it engineering rather than design.*

**Tb-5 — Honest absence beats fabricated presence.** Where the company has nothing to show, the hero shows nothing. §14.2's reasoning for deferring the About page is canonical: a thin one "actively damages credibility more than having no About page at all." The same governs placeholder metrics, illustrative client names, and stock team photography. A fabricated signal is a counter-signal waiting to be discovered. *This forbids fabrication, not effort — the hero's own claim, qualifier, and action are fully available.*

## 3. WHAT THE HERO CAN AND CANNOT BUILD

| Trust rank | Requires | Available? | Hero's contribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Past outcomes** | Named clients, real metrics, case studies | **No** | Establish credibility so these are believed on arrival |
| **2. Process rigor** | A described, repeatable method | **No** | Demonstrate rigor *behaviourally* — consistency, precision, correctness |
| **3. Technical depth** | Systems detail, demonstrable capability | **No** | Ch. 11's rungs 5–6: be a well-engineered artefact; use precise language |
| **4. Aesthetic polish** | Craft | **Yes** | Clear the hygiene bar so 1–3 get a fair hearing |

**The load-bearing cells are rows 2 and 3.** The hero cannot *describe* rigor or depth, but it can *exhibit* both — a surface consistent everywhere and correct at the edges is direct evidence of process rigor, of a kind that cannot be fabricated by a company that does not have it. The strongest trust position available to a hero with no proof content, and entirely a matter of engineering discipline.

## 4. THE COUNTER-SIGNAL CATALOGUE

Each row destroys more trust than any single positive signal builds.

| Counter-signal | Why severe | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| **A visible defect** — layout shift, broken wrap, misalignment at one width | Direct evidence against process rigor | Ch. 9, 29 |
| **A claim with no downstream support** | Discovered inconsistency; triggers re-evaluation of everything | Tb-2 |
| **A fabricated or placeholder proof element** | If detected, invalidates all remaining proof | Tb-5 |
| **A pressure device** | Signals need; reframes the vendor as motivated by the sale | Ch. 16 |
| **A stolen or modified scroll** | Removes control; prioritises effect over user | Ch. 6, 25 |
| **An uninvited interruption** — chat widget, overlay, exit-intent | Confirms the visitor's arrival suspicion exactly | Ch. 16, 25 |
| **An inaccessible path** — keyboard trap, invisible focus, poor contrast | Evidence of work that stopped at the happy path | Ch. 28 |
| **Stutter or jank in motion** | "Worse for the brand than no animation at all" | Ch. 21, 29 |
| **Self-descriptive adjectives** | Assertion in place of demonstration | Tb-1 |
| **Jargon** | Reads as concealment | Ch. 11, 18 |
| **A second CTA** | Signals an undecided vendor | Ch. 3, 27 |
| **Inconsistent treatment of identical elements** | Reads as an individual, not an organisation | Ch. 12 |

**How it is used.** A pre-review sweep, run *before* any positive trust proposal is considered. Most rows are engineering or QA findings rather than design decisions — which is this chapter's central practical claim: **for a hero with no proof content, trust is mostly built by the build.**

## 5. THE TRUST HANDOFF

```
 HERO establishes:             PROOF SECTIONS receive:
 Source credibility       ──►  Evidence read as coming from a
                               competent party — weighed, not discounted
 A specific claim         ──►  Something for the evidence to corroborate
                               (proof should "corroborate a claim the
                               visitor has already begun to believe")
 Zero counter-signals     ──►  A trust balance at neutral or better
 No spent proof           ──►  Full force available at first use
```

**If the handoff fails**, the proof sections start from a deficit, and each piece of evidence must first repair the hero's damage before building anything. The most expensive way to spend a case study.

---

## 6. DO / DON'T

**Do.** Treat §4 as the hero's primary trust deliverable — run it as a sweep before any positive trust proposal is entertained, and fix what it finds. Most rows are engineering defects, most are invisible in a design review, and each costs more than any badge or reassurance line could recover. The cheapest, highest-yield trust work available, and almost never scheduled as trust work.

**Don't.** Add an illustrative metric — "40% faster," "12 hours saved per week" — drawn from Master Vision's own examples. Those figures are explicitly illustrative placeholders, not real outcomes. Presenting one as fact fails Tb-5 and Ch. 27 item 7, and if a prospect asks which engagement produced it, the answer damages more than the number earned.

---

## 7. ANTI-PATTERNS

**Trust theatre.** Adding the visual vocabulary of trust — seals, badges, "as featured in," security icons, ratings — without the substance. Detected by asking, per device, what a visitor could verify if they tried. Cheap, conventional, briefly effective — and its eventual discovery as hollow triggers the asymmetry across the whole site.

**Proof front-loading.** Moving the strongest proof into the hero because the hero has the most traffic. Dangerous twice over: the proof lands where it cannot be interrogated, and is no longer available at full force where it would have converted well.

**Positive-signal accumulation.** Responding to a trust problem by adding trust signals without first running the counter-signal sweep. Detected when a hero has several trust-motivated additions and still fails the stress matrix.

## 10. STATUS

**The significant limitation.** This chapter is written for a site with no proof content — the honestly-stated current position. Several conclusions are *reinforced* by that scarcity rather than caused by it, but the reasoning should be re-examined when real proof exists. Tb-3 would still hold on sequencing grounds, but the argument would rest on that alone rather than on the currently-decisive fact that any hero proof would have to be fabricated.

UX Blueprint Ch. 53 is unwritten. When authored, it governs proof placement site-wide and this chapter inherits from it. If it concludes some minimal proof signal belongs above the fold, that outranks Tb-3 and this chapter should be amended rather than defended. This chapter's durable contribution is §4, which is unaffected either way.
