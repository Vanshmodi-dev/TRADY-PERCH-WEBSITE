# CHAPTER 27 — CTA PSYCHOLOGY

**Trady Perch Hero Experience Bible · Part VII: Behaviour & Response**
*First-canonical-draft. Implementation-independent.*

**Does Not Govern:** The conversion doctrine behind it (Ch. 16) or the copy register (Ch. 18).

---

## 1. THE POSITION

The CTA is the only element the visitor can act on, which makes it the element most likely to be argued about and most likely to be optimised into damage.

Two facts constrain it before any design begins. **It is Rung 3** — it depends on the claim and qualifier, and is meaningless before both. **It is G5** — last in the goal stack, pursued only where it does not damage the four goals above it. Neither ranking reflects its commercial importance, which is high; both reflect its *position in a dependency chain*, which is fixed.

What follows is the whole chapter: the hero's CTA must be **present, findable, honestly labelled, and unpressured** — and it must not be the most salient thing on the screen.

**The CTA's real job is to name a cost.** A call to action is usually discussed as an invitation. For a high-consideration purchase it is more accurately a **price tag on the next step**. At second ten the visitor is not asking "do I want this?" but "what does clicking cost me?" — in time, commitment, sales pressure, and the awkwardness of extracting themselves later. Every label answers that question whether or not it intends to:

- *"Get started"* implies onboarding — cheap in time, but mis-describes an engagement that begins with a conversation.
- *"Contact us"* implies a form, an unknown wait, an unspecified follow-up — vague cost, which reads as high.
- *"Book a Strategy Call"* implies a scheduled conversation of known length and purpose — a specific, bounded, verifiable cost.
- *"Talk to sales"* implies exactly what it says, and is honest, which is worth more than it sounds.

**The label that most accurately describes the cost converts best on this purchase**, because the buyer's dominant emotion is fear of a bad outcome and ambiguity about cost is itself a risk. This inverts the usual advice: friction-minimising labels underperform here, because they under-describe.

**Why the CTA must not be the loudest thing.** The single most common hero request is to increase its prominence. Refused, with three independent supports: **dependency** — as Rung 3, a dominant CTA means the eye reaches a decision before the information required to make it (Cg-3); **signalling** — a CTA that dominates reads as a company whose priority is the transaction (Bp-6); **scarcity** — §5.3 makes gold the only legitimate CTA colour, §6.1 caps it at ~10%, and §7.4 specifies "minimal in the hero," so a larger or more gold CTA spends the budget that gives the colour its meaning everywhere else.

The correct target: **found in under a second when looked for, and not found before it is looked for.** That is Rung 2, exactly.

**One action, and why the second is worse than nothing.** §5.2 forbids competing CTAs. Consolidated: **cognitively**, a second CTA is a decision requested before comprehension is complete, and an unresolved decision occupies working memory for the rest of the visit; **psychologically**, two CTAs read as a company that could not decide what to recommend; **attentionally**, two competing emphases produce no entry point and split a fixed budget; **practically**, the visitor who is not ready for the primary action does not need a second button — they need the rest of the page. That last point answers the request as it is usually framed: "some visitors aren't ready to book" is true, and it is an argument for the fourteen sections below, which exist precisely for those visitors.

## 2. CORE PRINCIPLES

**Ct-1 — Exactly one action.** Not two, not a primary with a secondary, not a text link alternative. *Navigation and the skip control are wayfinding and system affordances, not calls to action, and do not count. A prominent text link inviting a different action is a second CTA in a different costume.*

**Ct-2 — The label names the cost honestly.** It describes what actually happens next, with enough specificity that the visitor can price it. Also a trust obligation: a label that under-describes the commitment produces a visitor who feels misled at step two — a counter-signal delivered after they have already invested. *Specific and short are compatible; Master Vision's own examples are three and four words.*

**Ct-3 — Rung 2 saliency, never Rung 1.** The CTA is the second-most salient element, never the first. *Rung 2 means found second, without searching — a strong position, and where a well-designed CTA belongs. Not quiet, not hidden.*

**Ct-4 — Gold, once.** The CTA is gold, and it is the hero's single gold spend beyond the brand mark. §5.3 and §7.4 together produce the answer: gold appears on the mark and on the action, nowhere else. *Not a licence to enlarge the gold area for prominence — gold marks the action; it does not amplify it. Prominence is Ct-3's business and is bounded there.*

**Ct-5 — No pressure, in label or treatment.** No urgency, scarcity, exclamation, or implied deadline — in the label, adjacent copy, or behaviour. "Behaviour" is deliberate: a CTA that pulses, glows, or attracts attention on a timer applies pressure through motion rather than words, and violates Mo-2 besides. *Noticeable when looked for is required; insistent is forbidden.*

**Ct-6 — Always present, at every breakpoint.** Present and reachable at every viewport, never removed to save space. DSB Nv-3 states this for the navigation CTA explicitly — "never dropped from mobile to save space," with ordinary items compressing first — and the hero's CTA inherits the same standing. Mobile is where the temptation to drop it is highest and the visitor's need for it is unchanged. *Not a requirement that it be above the fold at every size; what is fixed is that it exists and is reachable without hunting.*

## 3. THE LABEL EVALUATION MATRIX

Criteria 1–5 are gates; a candidate failing any one is out of the comparison.

| # | Criterion | Question | Gate? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Honest** | Does it describe what actually happens next? | **Yes** |
| 2 | **Priceable** | Can the visitor estimate the time and commitment cost? | **Yes** |
| 3 | **Unpressured** | Free of urgency, scarcity, and exclamation? | **Yes** |
| 4 | **In register** | Same voice as the claim and qualifier? | **Yes** |
| 5 | **Relationship-framed** | A professional conversation, not a self-serve transaction? | **Yes** |
| 6 | **Action-oriented** | Begins with or centres a verb? | No |
| 7 | **Specific** | Distinguishable from a generic competitor's CTA? | No |
| 8 | **Economical** | Every word load-bearing? | No |

**Worked comparison, illustrative only.** *"Book a Strategy Call"* clears all five gates — honest (a call is booked), priceable (a scheduled conversation), unpressured, in register, relationship-framed. *"Get Started Free"* fails gate 1 (nothing starts, and nothing is free in a scope-based engagement), gate 2 (the cost is disguised), and gate 5 (self-serve framing). *"Act Now"* fails gate 3 outright and is forbidden by name in the source.

**Standing note on the actual label.** Master Vision leaves it explicitly unfinalised — "Book a Call" / "Start a Project," to be resolved in copy work. DSB Ch. 20 uses "Book a Strategy Call" twice as an illustrative example within a component spec: strong precedent, not a ratified content decision. This Bible does not resolve it; it supplies the matrix the resolution must pass.

## 4. WHAT CTA TREATMENT COMMUNICATES

| Treatment | Reads as | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| One CTA, gold, Rung 2 saliency | A single confident recommendation | Target |
| Two CTAs of similar weight | An undecided vendor | Forbidden (Ct-1) |
| CTA as the most salient element | Transaction-first; decision before comprehension | Forbidden (Ct-3) |
| CTA below the qualifier, clearly separated | Correct dependency order | Target |
| CTA with adjacent reassurance ("no obligation") | Draws attention to the risk it denies | Forbidden (Ch. 16) |
| CTA that pulses or animates on a timer | Pressure through motion | Forbidden (Ct-5, Mo-2) |
| CTA that moves position between states | Imprecise; the target shifts | Defect (Bp-3, Ix-3) |
| CTA absent on mobile | G5 failed for the majority of visitors | Forbidden (Ct-6) |
| Label that under-describes the commitment | Misleading; discovered at step two | Forbidden (Ct-2) |
| Generous hit area beyond the visible bounds | Considerate; easier for imprecise pointers and thumbs | Encouraged |

## 5. THE CTA IS NOT A CONVERSION LEVER

Stated separately because it is the position most often challenged.

The hero's CTA exists to make the next step **visible**, not to maximise the number of people who take it. Every available technique for increasing hero CTA clicks — more prominence, more gold, urgency, a second easier option, a magnetic pull, a lower-commitment label — damages a goal ranked above it.

The consequence for measurement is direct: **a rise in hero CTA click-through is not, by itself, evidence of improvement.** It is equally consistent with a hero that has begun pressuring under-informed visitors into calls they are not ready for — costing sales time and lowering close rates while looking like a win in the only dashboard that reports on the hero. Ms-1 governs; Chapter 14's §3 procedure is how a proposal here is adjudicated.

---

## 6. DO / DON'T

**Do.** Choose the label that most accurately prices the next step, even when a vaguer one would convert more clicks. Accuracy is what makes the click *worth something*: a visitor who booked a strategy call knowing it is a scheduled conversation arrives prepared; one who clicked "Get started" expecting a product arrives confused and leaves annoyed. On a purchase where the dominant fear is choosing the wrong partner, the CTA is the first place the company demonstrates that it says what it means.

**Don't.** Add a secondary "or see how it works" link beneath the primary CTA. It is the most reasonable-sounding violation of Ct-1 and it fails on four independent grounds — a decision before comprehension, an indecision signal, a split attention budget, and a duplicate of what the scroll already provides. The visitor who wants to see how it works is about to scroll into a page built to show them.

## 7. ANTI-PATTERNS

**Prominence escalation.** The CTA growing across releases — larger, then bolder, then more gold, then with a glow — because each review finds it insufficiently noticed. Detected by comparing releases. An element strengthened three times has a hierarchy problem, not a saliency one: the claim above it is probably failing, so the visitor never reaches the point of wanting an action.

**The friction-minimising label.** Replacing an honest label with a lower-commitment one to raise clicks. Detected at gates 1 and 2. It raises clicks and lowers the quality of every resulting conversation — a metric-capture failure with a downstream cost invisible to the metric.

**Reassurance accretion.** Microcopy accumulating beneath the CTA — "no obligation," "free consultation," "we'll respond within 24 hours." Each is well-intentioned; together they read as a company arguing with an objection nobody raised, and the first two draw attention to risks the label had already priced honestly.

**The mobile drop.** Removing or burying the CTA at small viewports because space is scarce. Detected at 320px and in small landscape. Ct-6 and Nv-3 both forbid it: ordinary elements compress first.

## 8. ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA

- [ ] Exactly one CTA, at every breakpoint and variant. *(Ct-1)*
- [ ] The label passes all five gates in §3. *(Ct-2)*
- [ ] The CTA holds Rung 2 saliency, verified by the blur test. *(Ct-3, At-1)*
- [ ] Gold appears on the mark and the CTA, nowhere else in the hero. *(Ct-4, Lx-1)*
- [ ] No urgency, scarcity, exclamation, or timed attention behaviour. *(Ct-5)*
- [ ] The CTA is present and reachable at 320px, in small landscape, and at 200% zoom. *(Ct-6)*
- [ ] No reassurance microcopy accompanies it. *(§4, §7)*
- [ ] The CTA does not move position between any two states. *(§4)*
- [ ] Hit area is generous beyond the visible bounds. *(§4)*
- [ ] Any click-through change is reported alongside comprehension and scroll quality. *(§5)*
